# Introduction and Aim

All objects in the universe experience a gravitational force of attraction towards Earth, and the closer to the Earth’s centre they are, the stronger the force is. This force creates an acceleration on the object, towards the centre of the Earth (generally taken as the direction), and this acceleration will be a constant at a specific location on Earth and can be calculated. This is the acceleration due to gravity and the aim of this report is to calculate the value of this in Cape Town by observing the 1D aspect (the direction) of the motion of objects which accelerate towards the Earth upon being released at some height

# Discussion and Conclusion

From this it can concluded that _g = -9.57717 ± 0.04410_ in Cape Town (as shown under the analysis section). The aim was fulfilled because although 9.8 (the generally stated _g_ for a frictionless world) is not within this range, the best estimate is slightly less than this which is what is expected in a scenario involving friction.

# Sources of Uncertainty

*   The limited frame rate and quality of a video affects the quality of the results.
*   The judgement in marking the same spot on the object in the Tracker software (such as the centre of mass of a ball)
*   The sensitivity of the Tracker software to calculate values with a limited precision (due to memory)

# Recommendations

*   Provide some indication on the objects that can be used to mark the position of the objects on the Tracker software, such as a red sticker at the middle of a ball. This will allow for greater precision, with less uncertainty associated with the judgement in marking the same spot on the object in the Tracker software
*   Drop the object with minimal wind (if wind is present) and drop the object without any sort of spin so as to eliminate using some indication which is not the centre of mass which can be used in the Tracker software (such as a bottle lid)
*   Film the videos with a greater frame rate and quality (for the same reasons as above)
